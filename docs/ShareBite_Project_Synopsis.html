1. Project Abstract (Simple Summary)

Every day, large quantities of fresh, good-quality food are thrown away by restaurants, banquet halls, wedding caterers, and bakeries. At the same time, millions of underprivileged people, homeless individuals, and children go to sleep hungry. The main reason this happens is not a shortage of food, but a lack of communication and quick connection between the people who have extra food and the NGOs or charities that can distribute it.

ShareBite is an easy-to-use web platform that solves this problem in real time. Whenever a restaurant or caterer has extra food, they can post it on ShareBite in less than 30 seconds using automatic GPS location. Nearby verified NGOs and volunteers instantly see the food on an interactive map. An NGO volunteer can claim the food batch and receive a secure 6-digit OTP code. When the volunteer reaches the restaurant, the donor checks this OTP before handing over the food. To ensure that nobody ever receives spoiled food, an automatic safety timer checks all listings every minute and immediately removes any food that has passed its safe expiry time.

Core Benefit: ShareBite connects food donors with nearby NGOs in under 12 minutes, preventing food waste, stopping food poisoning, and feeding hungry people.

2. Problem Statement (Why This Project is Needed)

Currently, donating surplus food is difficult because of several real-world issues:

  • Food Spoils Quickly: Cooked food stays fresh for only 3 to 5 hours. In traditional systems, people make phone calls or send WhatsApp messages, which takes too long, and the food often goes bad before anyone collects it.
  • Donors Don't Know Nearby NGOs: A restaurant manager rarely knows which nearby charity or soup kitchen has free volunteers or hungry people to feed right now.
  • Safety & Trust Issues: Without a verification system, anyone could take the food for the wrong reasons, and donors worry about liability or food safety.
  • No Automatic Expiry Control: Ordinary websites do not automatically remove old food, which can cause food poisoning if expired food is consumed.

3. Objectives of the Project

The key goals of ShareBite are:

  1. Quick Food Posting (Under 30 Seconds): Allow restaurants and donors to easily post extra food with quantity (servings/kg/boxes), food type (Veg/Non-Veg), and safe expiry time.
  2. Live Map & Nearby NGO Search: Show all available food batches and nearby NGOs on an interactive map using real-time GPS within an adjustable radius of 2 to 50 km.
  3. Safe Pickup Using 6-Digit OTP: Protect the food from theft or wrong delivery by generating a unique 6-digit OTP when a food batch is claimed. The donor enters this OTP before handing over the food.
  4. Automatic Food Safety Timer: Run an automatic check every 60 seconds. If a food item reaches its safe-by expiry time, the system automatically marks it as expired so no one can claim spoiled food.
  5. Turn-by-Turn Driving Directions: Show the volunteer the best driving route, distance (km), and estimated travel time (minutes) to reach the donor's location quickly.
  6. Measurable Impact & Certificates: Automatically track the total number of meals saved and kilograms of food rescued, and generate a printable Certificate of Impact for donors.

5.1 Main Modules of ShareBite

  • Food Posting Module: Donors enter the food title, whether it is Veg or Non-Veg, quantity, and when it was prepared. The app uses GPS to fill the address automatically.
  • Nearby Map & Search Module: Shows orange markers for food batches, blue markers for NGOs, and green markers for restaurants. Users can filter by distance (2 km to 50 km).
  • OTP Pickup Verification Module: Generates a random 6-digit code for the NGO. The donor enters this code to confirm that the real volunteer has collected the food.
  • Automated Food Safety Module: Checks all food listings every minute. If any food is past its safe consumption time, it is removed from the map automatically.
  • Admin Dashboard Module: Allows college or municipal supervisors to check total meals saved and verify new NGOs and donors with one click.

6. Key Formulas Used in the Project

6.1 Real Distance Calculation (Haversine Formula)

To find the exact distance between the user's phone GPS and nearby restaurants or NGOs across the curve of the Earth, ShareBite uses the Haversine Formula:

Distance (d) = 2 × R × arcsin( √( sin²(Δlat / 2) + cos(lat1) × cos(lat2) × sin²(Δlon / 2) ) )
where Earth Radius R = 6,371 km

This allows the app to sort food listings by distance instantly in less than 20 milliseconds.

6.2 Food Rescued and Carbon Savings Formula

When food rots in landfills, it creates harmful greenhouse gases. ShareBite calculates total meals saved and carbon prevented:

Total Meals Saved = Sum of all servings distributed
Total Rescued Weight (kg) = (Servings × 0.40 kg) + (Boxes × 2.50 kg) + direct kg
Carbon Prevented (kg CO₂e) = Total Rescued Weight (kg) × 2.50 kg CO₂e / kg

8. Expected Outcomes & Social Value

What ShareBite Delivers:
  • A Complete Working Web Application: Runs smoothly on laptops, tablets, and mobile phones with zero complicated setup.
  • Feeds Hungry People: Moves fresh, safe food from banquet halls and restaurants straight to hungry children and poor families.

9. Conclusion & Future Scope

ShareBite shows that modern web technologies and open-source map services can make food recovery fast, secure, and transparent. It solves the real-world communication delay between donors and charities. In the future, we plan to add:

  • AI Food Waste Prediction: Using machine learning to help restaurants predict how much extra food they might have based on past weekend sales and weather.
  • Smart Temperature Sensors: Using small IoT sensors inside delivery boxes to make sure food stays cool and fresh during delivery.
  • Multi-Stop Route Optimization: Helping an NGO van collect food from 3 or 4 restaurants in one efficient trip.
